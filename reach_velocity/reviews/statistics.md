## Adversarial review: statistics of the Middle-reach GP chain

Checks were run read-only from `reach_velocity` with `../.venv/bin/python`; scripts and outputs are in `<reviewer scratch space, not kept>` (`check_dayout.py`, `nested_law.py`, `quick_checks.py`, `check2.py`, `*.out`).

### Findings, most severe first

**1. The ADCP contradicts the field by a factor 2.3, and the logbook says the level agrees.** (a) Entry 16: "the level agrees either way at mid-stage (A 0.098 against 0.094)"; entry 21 lists "the ADCP follows the same law independently" as one of three reasons to believe the stage law. (b) Figure 03's own title: bias +108 mm/s, coverage 0.25, obs/pred 0.58. Under the adopted law (0.45, 1.0) the field's A at the ADCP position is 0.26 (sd log 0.20); the record sits at 0.44 of the prediction (column reading), 0.30 (point reading); 0% of the season inside the field's 95% band. Model-free version: on 2026-06-18 at stage 0.748 m, profiles 1.7, 3.4 and 4.9 m from the ADCP (0618-10/09/11) give a = 0.074–0.087; the ADCP at stage 0.73–0.76 gives 0.039 (column) or 0.021 (point). Its exponent on (stage − 0.45) is 0.63 (column) or 1.26 (point), bracketing the law's 1.0. (c) The "one campaign + one sensor" claim leans on the ADCP as the continuous check of the chain; at present it validates neither level nor exponent, and "0.11 residual sd with level fitted" is shape-only with the reading chosen post hoc. (d) Resolve what 'Vb' is before anything else: a single slanted-beam velocity of a 25° instrument is 0.42 of the along-stream speed, suspiciously close to 0.44. Until then remove the ADCP from the validation table and from entry 21's three reasons, and report the like-for-like number above.

**2. The nest test detects "nests are at the bank", not the velocity chain.** (a) Entry 20: AUC 0.72, KS p = 1e-9, "the field says where the slow water is". (b) Re-scoring the same 59 nests with distance-to-bank alone gives AUC 0.76 (KS p = 3e-10); log A alone 0.74; depth alone 0.50. Nests' median bank distance is 3.4 m versus 11.4 m for the reach's cells. Dependence: 49 of 59 nests lie within 10 m of another nest; 17 come from one day; with day as the unit (9 days) the mean percentile is 0.36, t-test p = 0.11, Wilcoxon p = 0.16. (c) The ecological deliverable's only external check is currently a restatement of a known habitat preference, and the p-value assumes 59 independent draws. (d) Report the bank-distance null beside the model; test velocity *conditional* on bank distance (stratify by bank-distance bin, or conditional logistic used-vs-available with both covariates); use cluster-level (day or 10 m cluster) inference; reword the claim accordingly.

**3. The extrapolation band is the spread of three hand-picked laws, not a posterior, and it narrows outside the surveyed range.** (a) `uncertainty.py` LAW_RIDGE = three (h0, γ) pairs drawn uniformly; entry 17 calls the law's 0.00 in-range contribution "the extrapolation cost made explicit". (b) The grid (`step3b_law_grid.csv`) is flat within optimiser noise: the adopted law scores 0.3093 with two restarts and 0.3165 with one (`check2.out`), larger than the gaps between ridge members. (0.65, 0.5) at 0.317 is excluded from the ridge yet gives 1.84x at 1.5 m against 2.11–2.51x for the ridge. `step7_sensitivity.csv`: 90% half-width of the reach share is 3.9 points at 0.90 m and 3.1 at 1.30 m, i.e. narrower above the range; the logbook's "±5 inside, ±8 above" is not in the files. 35% of the 2025 record and 50% of the 2026 record (25% above 1.09 m) lie outside the surveyed stage band. (c) The May 2026 "5 to 20% slow water" and the 2026 season median 0.39 rest on this plus the wetted-width cap. (d) Give (h0, γ) a real distribution (day-bootstrap of the day-out CRPS surface, or a likelihood over day offsets), constrain h0 < 0.66 m because the river flowed at 0.66 m in both years, and quote season numbers only for in-range stages (2025: 0.42, not 0.47) with the extrapolated fraction flagged.

**4. Day and place are confounded in the stage-law identification; the within-place evidence goes the other way.** (a) Entry 16: "the day effect is stage", Spearman 0.85 on 11 days. (b) The two highest days are single transects (AT0401, AT05). With no law, the 06-10 offset is +0.57 although AT03, 3 m away, has the same raw amplitude (log ratio 0.03): the "offset" is the GP averaging AT03 with the slow 2026-06-18 transects at −2 and +54 m. At AT03/AT05 amplitude did not rise over 0.20 m of stage where the law says +0.37; AT07's revisit rises more than the law. Nested CV passes (0.310 vs 0.309), so selection optimism is not the problem; identifiability is. (c) The law is the paper's transfer mechanism. (d) Make the discharge argument primary: measured section Q is place-free by continuity and follows Q = 28.1(stage − 0.45)^1.70 with 7% scatter; state the pairs as the unresolved limitation.

**5. "Share of wetted width" is a share of points.** (a) Entries 19–20. (b) `section_stats`/`sample_series` average over points; with 2.4–7 m spacing and half-weight edge points, the spacing-weighted share on the survey day is lower by 0.069 on average (0.45 vs 0.39 over sections), larger than the reported band. (c) The ecological number is biased by more than its interval. (d) Trapezoid weights per draw, with the margin to the bank line included.

**6. The six-feature ARD model is not distinguishable from bank distance alone, and the ablation is irreproducible.** (b) Paired per-transect CRPS (`step2_predictions.csv`): full+notes minus generic shape −0.014, better on 11/20, Wilcoxon p = 0.37 (versus the reach mean: 17/20, p < 0.001). No script in the repository produces `step8_feature_ablation.csv` (grep finds nothing; timestamp 00:20 vs 00:14), and `reach_velocity/` is entirely untracked in git. (d) Commit the folder; regenerate the ablation from a script with a paired test; consider presenting the one-feature model as the model and ARD as the check.

**7. The discrepancy term is noise and is injected as iid per-point noise.** (b) q95|z| = 2.07 on 257 points clustered in 11 days; day-cluster bootstrap 1.81–2.27, inflation 0.92–1.16. iid injection shrinks by √n in section and reach means. Empirically the held-out-section 90% band still covers 20/21 sections (section-level z sd 0.8, `check_dayout.out`). (d) Drop the term or make it a per-draw reach-level factor with the day-offset sd (0.12–0.13); calibrate on the quantity reported (section mean), which the check shows is covered.

**8. Modelled discharge is computed inconsistently.** (b) `step6_continuity.csv`: Q_model = mean_v × area is below the measured Σvhw on 18/20 sections by 8–15% (velocity–depth covariance). The reach exponent 1.70 is conditional on h0 = 0.45 borrowed from the amplitude law. (d) Σ v_i h_i w_i per draw; fit h0.

**9. Mislabelled and flattering numbers.** The validation table's readings MAE/coverage (0.083/0.906) and "oracle 0.304" are Q^0.75 results; the adopted law gives 0.081/0.897 (same within rounding, but label it). "Spread 0.153" is an sd; the comparable MAD is 0.123 and the day-mean oracle 0.115, so skill is ~30%, not ~45%.

**10. Map internals.** Bed noise is drawn iid per cell (entry 14 promised joint bed samples; between-transect bed sd is 0.17 m); notes set to population shares make map–section agreement a consistency check, not validation.

### Strong; do not touch
The continuity check and reach rating (3–12% same-day agreement, place-free Q(stage), the gauge-vs-sensor storm days); the transect-out transfer versus the reach mean; the day-out design and its point- and section-level coverage (0.95 both); the CRPS formula and held-out measurement-noise handling; the RTK datum tie by session; the stage cleaning against night readings; the label/height hygiene; the candid framing of the map as illustration; the Upper reach as a negative control.
