What the study is: a hand-held velocity survey of a river reach (Huron River, Michigan; 20 cross-sections, 257 locations, 11 days over two seasons; 3 to 5 readings per location at heights above the bed), one ultrasonic stage sensor, one bed-mounted ADCP at one point, RTK cross-sections. The chain: log-law amplitude per location -> a 'conveyance' A = a / (stage - h0)^1 that removes the day's stage -> a Gaussian process over geometric features (ARD Matern 3/2, WLS mean on substrate notes, per-point noise from the readings' spread) -> the field on a 2 m grid with a bed GP -> Monte Carlo through bed, stage, law, z0 and a calibrated discrepancy -> the depth-averaged velocity of every section every two hours over two seasons ('spatial rating curves') -> the share of the reach below an ecological velocity threshold (0.2 m/s, smallmouth bass nesting) over time, checked against 63 nest sites. Validation: transect held out, day held out, readings on held-out days, the ADCP through the season, continuity of discharge between sections and against the USGS gauge. Step 8 applied the same tests to two other reaches without a sensor.

The intended paper: one campaign plus one stage sensor gives season-long, uncertainty-bounded velocity fields and an ecological number; for a hydroinformatics readership (Environmental Modelling & Software or Water Resources Research).
