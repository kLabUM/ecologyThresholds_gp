# Review of `reach_velocity` (read-only; checks printed numbers only, nothing written)

## Findings, most severe first

**1. Reach-level bands are about half their defensible width: every reach-wide error is drawn independently per section.**
(a) "The band on the share is about ±5 points" (entry 20). (b) `uncertainty.py::sample_series` draws the discrepancy iid per point (`rng.normal(0, chain.disc_sd, npts)`), and stage noise, z0 and the GP posterior separately per section; steps 6–7 then length-weight 20 independent sections, so common errors average away. But the residual day level after the law is a common factor with sd 0.13 in log (entry 16). Rerunning the chain with one joint GP draw over all 257 points, one stage/z0 draw, and the discrepancy split into a common 0.13 plus iid 0.14: reach share at 0.90 m goes from 0.40 [0.36, 0.43] as run to 0.40 [0.30, 0.49]; at 0.75 m from 0.63 [0.59, 0.68] to 0.62 [0.50, 0.77]. The joint GP alone changes little; the common term doubles the band. (c) "Uncertainty-bounded" is the paper's claim. (d) Draw the reach jointly in `sample_series` (predict_cov on all points; common stage, z0, day-level discrepancy), rerun 25/26, and add a section-level coverage check (hold out each day, score its measured section-mean velocity against the band); `uncertainty_calibration.json` (0.946) is point-level only.

**2. The headline seasonal contrast is extrapolation under an under-sampled law.**
(a) "May 2026 ... 5 to 20% slow water; June 2025 ... about 40%" (entry 20). (b) 25% of 2026 hours and 92% of April 2026 hours lie above the surveyed 1.09 m (`step7_reach_series.csv`). The chain samples only the (h0, γ) ridge, which at 1.50 m gives amplitude 2.1–2.5× its 0.90 m value; the ADCP under the column reading, which entry 9 and `amplitude_inputs.json` call the instrument's geometry, gives h^0.46, a factor 1.58. The day-out test that chose the law separates candidates by 0.02 CRPS on 11 days (0.309 vs 0.329). The band above the range (0.05) is narrower than inside (0.07) only because the share is near zero, and wetted width cannot grow (entry 20). (c) The most quotable number is the least supported. (d) Add the depth^0.46 law to the law draw above 1.09 m or show it as a scenario; hatch out-of-range hours in Fig 5; quote May 2026 as a lower bound with a range; state the 0.02 CRPS margin.

**3. The ADCP is not an independent temporal validation.**
(a) "the ADCP follows the same law independently" (entry 21); table row "ADCP season, reach law with level fitted (point reading) 0.110". (b) `step3b_adcp_shape.json`: under the column reading the reach law leaves a trend of −0.27 per log depth (sd 0.13) while depth^0.5 fits with sd 0.075; figure 03 bottom-left: the field's A predicts the record with bias +108 mm/s, coverage 0.25. The level is fitted and the shape agrees only under the reading the logbook rejects; the SonTek Vb question is open. (c) Referees will read the ADCP as the one continuous check. (d) Settle the instrument model; show the ADCP as a shape check under both readings with level fitted; drop "independently".

**4. The nest test validates bank distance, not the velocity field.**
(a) "AUC 0.72, KS p 1e-9" (entry 20). (b) Same construction with other scores (from `grid.csv`, `step7_nests.csv`): distance to nearest bank alone AUC 0.76; conveyance A without stage 0.74; depth 0.50. Nests sit 3.4 m from the bank against 11.4 m for wet cells; crews searched wadeable margins. (c) Any model that says banks are slow scores 0.7. (d) Report the baselines beside the model; test conditionally (among cells within 5 m of a bank, are nests in the slower ones?) or present nests descriptively.

**5. One reach in three falsifies the spatial model; the stage law is untested outside the Middle.**
(a) "The Lower behaves like the Middle ... the day levels vary with flow" (entry 21). (b) `step8_summary.csv`: Upper transect-out CRPS 0.429 vs reach mean 0.378, hit rate 69% vs base 64% (worse than a constant). Lower day level vs local level: 0.3 per m, Spearman 0.46 (n 7), against the Middle's 3.2 per m, 0.84. (c) The title claim is shown once; a second reach shows the field, not the law; a third shows neither. (d) One table for three reaches; present the Upper as the method's floor (amplitude 0.045 vs noise 0.011, wood and weed) plus the design lessons (interleave sections across days, repeat sections, a sensor per reach). Do not say the Lower confirms the law.

**6. "Velocity fields" outruns the evidence.**
(a) Brief: "season-long velocity fields". (b) sd of log A 0.31 between transects vs 0.23 on them; "the field is a guess between transects" (entry 18); entry 11's own verdict. (c) PHABSIM/River2D readers hold a field to 2-D standards. (d) Claim spatial rating curves on 20 sections plus a reach integral; show the map once, with its sd panel.

**7. The validation table double-counts.**
(a) Fifteen rows in `step6_validation_table.csv`. (b) 6 of 11 days hold out exactly one transect (`step8_day_levels.csv`), so day-out and transect-out share folds and the oracle 0.304 equals transect-out 0.307 by construction; continuity at the survey stage is in-sample (`25_step6_rating_curves.py` builds Q_measured from the training a and h). (c) A referee counts three distinct tests: transect-out, law choice, same-day continuity (3–12%) with the 0.77 gauge ratio. (d) Collapse to those with n and the null beaten, and add the practitioner number the ±5-point band hides: held-out readings called below/above 0.2 m/s correctly 76% vs a 58% base rate (`step3_readings_heldout.csv`).

**8. The ecological number is under-defined.**
(a) "0.47 (2025), 0.39 (2026)". (b) `section_stats` counts wet surveyed points, not width; margins beyond the last point are excluded; near-bed reference adds 8–10 points; 0.10/0.20/0.30 m/s give 0.16/0.37/0.62 at 0.90 m; nests measure 0.02 m/s where the cell says 0.15 (entry 20). (c) Definitional choices move it more than its band. (d) Define once with the ecology cited (height, threshold, width integral to the waterline from the bed GP); call it a section-scale quantity that does not resolve shelter; give the variants in one sentence.

**9. Stale statements.** Budget row 13 says April–May 2026 missing; coverage is 99% and 89% (gaps are late May–June and August). Figures 00t/00u/00r/00s and entry 12 use A = a/√h and "β = 1/2 from the ADCP"; the adopted law is a = A(stage − 0.45)^1. Regenerate any that enter the paper.

**10. Reproducibility.** Good: `run_all.sh`, fixed seeds, raw copies. Missing: a pinned environment for this folder (`requirements.txt` is ranged; sklearn's GP optimiser shifts across versions), runtime, provenance of the centreline/bank files, a data statement, and crew first names in `amplitude_checks.json` and figure 00o (anonymise). The 2025-05-27 nests borrow 30 May's stage; say so.

## Strong; do not touch
Units converted once at load with named corrections (entry 7). The log-law reduction with per-location uncertainty and the 30 mm/s transfer check. The night-referenced stage correction. The RTK datum tie through one water-surface slope (5 cm/100 m, residual 1.3 cm), a result on its own. The target: one static conveyance field, stage through a law, geometry measured. The validation design (transect-out, day-out, random folds as the cautionary case, CRPS and coverage in physical units, the oracle bound). The ARD finding that the field is bank distance plus pool/riffle depth. Map-vs-sections agreement. The storm days where the gauge misled and the sensor did not.

For the reader's first questions: a 2-D model needs continuous bathymetry and calibrated roughness and returns no interval, and the bed is known on 20 lines 30–60 m apart; the GP is the interpolator of one measured field with known noise, not a learner of physics; what transfers is the recipe: log-law amplitude with one z0, a Manning-like law (ridge member h0 0.55, γ 0.75 is Manning's 2/3 with the ADCP's h0 0.63; say this) checked by day-out, a GP on two geometric features, a joint uncertainty chain, and the survey design.
